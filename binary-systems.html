Two-state magnetic systems

Counting microstates, total magnetic moments, and multiplicities.

The model and its states

Consider \(N\) distinguishable, noninteracting magnetic moments. Each has a projection \(+\mu\) or \(-\mu\) along a chosen axis. A microstate specifies the orientation of every moment; there are \(2^N\) such microstates. Here \(M\) denotes the total magnetic moment along that axis, not momentum or magnetization per unit volume.

Let \(N_{\uparrow}\) and \(N_{\downarrow}\) count the two orientations:

\[N_{\uparrow}+N_{\downarrow}=N\]

\[2s=N_{\uparrow}-N_{\downarrow}\]

\[M=2\mu s\]

Thus \(s\) runs from \(-N/2\) to \(N/2\) in steps of one, giving \(N+1\) possible values of \(M\). For even \(N\), \(s\) is an integer and includes zero. For odd \(N\), \(s\) is a half-integer and zero is not allowed.

Multiplicity: how many microstates?

Choosing which \(N_{\uparrow}\) moments point up gives the multiplicity \(g\):

\[g(N,s)=\binom{N}{N_{\uparrow}}\]

\[g(N,s)=\frac{N!}{N_{\uparrow}!\,N_{\downarrow}!}\]

The populations in the denominator are

\[N_{\uparrow}=\frac{N}{2}+s\]

\[N_{\downarrow}=\frac{N}{2}-s\]

Both populations are integers, including when \(s\) is a half-integer.

Example: four moments

  • \(N_{\uparrow}=0\): \(s=-2\), \(M=-4\mu\), \(g=1\).
  • \(N_{\uparrow}=1\): \(s=-1\), \(M=-2\mu\), \(g=4\).
  • \(N_{\uparrow}=2\): \(s=0\), \(M=0\), \(g=6\).
  • \(N_{\uparrow}=3\): \(s=1\), \(M=2\mu\), \(g=4\).
  • \(N_{\uparrow}=4\): \(s=2\), \(M=4\mu\), \(g=1\).

The multiplicities sum to \(16=2^4\). There are five possible total moments, but sixteen microstates.

When is multiplicity a probability?

If all \(2^N\) microstates are equally likely,

\[P(s)=\frac{g(N,s)}{2^N}\]

This assumption is appropriate for the noninteracting model at zero field. It is not generally valid in a nonzero field at finite temperature, where different orientations have different energies and require the appropriate Boltzmann weights.

A useful large-N approximation

For large \(N\) and \(s\) near the center of the distribution, the binomial multiplicity has the Gaussian approximation:

\[g(N,s)\approx 2^N\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi N}}\,e^{-2s^2/N}\]

This is an approximation to the central region, not an exact formula or a reliable description near complete alignment. Use the exact binomial expression for small \(N\) or tail probabilities.

Check your understanding

  • For \(N=3\), list the allowed \(s\) values and their multiplicities. Why is \(M=0\) impossible?
  • Explain why equal probabilities for individual microstates do not imply equal probabilities for the \(N+1\) total moments.
  • For \(N=4\) with equiprobable microstates, the probability of \(M=0\) is \(6/16\), not \(1/5\).
