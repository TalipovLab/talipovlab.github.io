Our research

Molecules, mechanisms, and function

We connect electronic structure and molecular motion with chemical reactivity and function, using quantum chemistry, molecular simulation, and models that make the results chemically understandable.

From an unusual bond in a small molecule to the response of a protein or membrane, our aim is to explain how molecular structure gives rise to observable behavior. Close connections between theory and experiment guide this work.

The themes below bring together contributions from our group, earlier research, and collaborations. Representative studies illustrate the science; the complete publication catalogue provides the full record.

Bonding

Understanding unusual chemical bonds

Small molecules can challenge familiar pictures of a chemical bond. An exceptionally long bond or an unexpected electronic structure gives us a way to examine how electron sharing, resonance, and correlation hold molecules together.

Our approach

We combine electronic-structure calculations with valence-bond and electron-density analyses. Alongside numerical calculations, we develop symbolic models that make the relationship between molecular structure and bonding easier to interpret.

Connecting a surprising structure to a bonding model

Our work on HOON predicted an unusually long oxygen–oxygen bond, subsequently characterized by microwave spectroscopy. Studies of aromatic stabilization in a hydrogen-transfer transition state connect bonding concepts with reactivity. The symvb package brings this chemical perspective into analytical models and worked examples.

Organic electronics

Connecting molecular structure with electronic behavior

Adding or removing an electron can change a conjugated molecule far beyond a single bond. We study where that charge resides, how the molecular framework responds, and how these changes appear in redox and optical measurements.

Our approach

Quantum-chemical calculations and multistate models let us separate the effects of connectivity, end groups, and structural relaxation. Collaboration with experimental groups connects these molecular explanations with electrochemical and spectroscopic observations.

Explaining localization and delocalization

Our studies show how chain length, cyclic topology, and terminal groups shape charge distributions in organic molecular wires. The multistate parabolic model provides a common framework for interpreting these effects, including negative-charge distributions across several families of conjugated oligomers.

Reactions & catalysis

Explaining reaction pathways and selectivity

A reaction can have several plausible pathways, yet produce a particular product under the conditions of an experiment. Understanding that choice requires a molecular account of the intermediates, transition states, and bonds that change along the way.

Our approach

We use quantum-chemical calculations to compare mechanisms and interpret the electronic changes behind bond activation. Working with experimental collaborators allows proposed pathways to be considered alongside observed products, kinetic evidence, and the properties of reactive intermediates.

Tracing the chemistry behind a transformation

Collaborative studies have examined selective carbon–carbon bond cleavage in ruthenium-catalyzed coupling and carbon–nitrogen bond activation by a high-valent cobalt complex. Work on the Scholl reaction, endoperoxidation, and oxidation chemistry extends this approach to other transformations and questions of selectivity.

Biomolecular chemistry

Understanding recognition and reactivity in proteins

A protein is more than a binding site: its shape, motion, and electrostatic environment influence which molecules it recognizes and how they react. We investigate these connections to understand enzyme function, selectivity, and biological redox chemistry.

Our approach

Our work combines molecular docking, molecular dynamics, and electronic-structure calculations. Comparisons with biochemical and structural experiments help connect a proposed molecular interaction to its consequences for recognition or catalysis.

From the binding environment to chemical function

Studies of DUSP5 phosphatase examine how its binding pockets and conserved residues contribute to substrate recognition and enzymatic activity. In complementary work on S-nitrosothiols, bonding models explain how a protein environment can shift the balance of resonance structures and influence reactivity.

Membranes & materials

Linking molecular organization to material properties

The behavior of a membrane or material emerges from the organization and motion of its molecular components. We study these connections across scales, from the flexibility of lipid bilayers to selective transport through composite membranes.

Our approach

Coarse-grained molecular dynamics provides a bridge between molecular composition and collective membrane behavior. In collaborative materials studies, molecular modeling complements experimental measurements of structure, transport, and photochemical performance.

Making the connection across scales

Our membrane study with Sam Brown and Jessica Pallarez compares bending moduli in single-component lipid bilayers. Related collaborative work examines selective proton transport in graphene–polymer composites, carbon nanotubes, and photocatalytic conversion of plastics. These systems share an interest in relating microscopic structure to measurable function.

Computational methods

Building interpretable and reliable molecular models

A useful calculation depends on the model and the numerical methods behind it. We examine how molecular properties are represented, how simulation data are analyzed, and which assumptions limit the conclusions that can be drawn.

Our approach

Our methods work combines physical models, numerical analysis, and comparisons with reference calculations or observations. We investigate both how to obtain a result and how sensitive that result is to the information and approximations used to construct it.

Understanding what the calculation can tell us

Studies of electrostatic charge fitting expose why different fitted charges can reproduce similar molecular potentials. Work on the weighted histogram analysis method improves the numerical solution used to reconstruct free-energy profiles. Contributions on docking scores and approximate quantum-mechanical protein simulations address related questions about model accuracy and interpretation.
