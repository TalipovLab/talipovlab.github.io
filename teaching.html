Explore a model

An electron in a box

How do confinement and quantum state change an electron’s energy? Adjust the box length and quantum number. The plot shows relative probability density along the box.

Interactive example

Model: an electron in a one-dimensional infinite square well. \(E_n=\frac{n^2h^2}{8m_eL^2}\). Position is scaled by L and density by its maximum, so changing L changes the energy while preserving the plotted shape for a given n.

Predict, then test

  • At a fixed box length, what happens to the energy when n changes from 1 to 2? Predict a factor of four, then test it.
  • At a fixed n, double the box length. The energy should fall to one quarter of its original value.
  • Count the interior nodes: there are n − 1. The plotted density is divided by its maximum, so this display does not show the change in absolute density when the box length changes.

This idealized model develops intuition about confinement; it does not describe all of the interactions in a real molecule.
